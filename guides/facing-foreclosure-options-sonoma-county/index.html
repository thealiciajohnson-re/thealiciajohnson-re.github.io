Facing Foreclosure in Sonoma County? Here Are Your Real Options

If you've fallen behind, take a breath: you have more options than you think, and California law is on your side. Here's the honest rundown.

Short answer

A missed payment or even a Notice of Default is not the end of the road. Your options include working with your loan servicer on a loan modification, repayment plan, or forbearance; a short sale if you owe more than the home is worth; or selling before the auction to protect the equity you've built. Free counseling is available, and acting early keeps the most doors open.

Your options, plainly

  • Talk to your servicer first. California's Homeowner Bill of Rights requires lenders to pause foreclosure while reviewing a loan-modification application. You may qualify for a modification, a repayment plan, or forbearance — especially after a disaster like fire or flood.
  • Sell before the auction to protect your equity. If your home is worth more than you owe, selling lets you pay off the loan and keep your equity instead of losing it in a foreclosure. This is the option many homeowners don't realize they still have.
  • Short sale. If you owe more than it's worth, a short sale with lender approval is usually far better for your credit than a completed foreclosure.
  • Free counseling. HUD-approved, bilingual foreclosure-prevention counseling is available locally through Fair Housing Advocates of Northern California, at no cost.

A California law that can buy you time: AB 2424

As of 2025, AB 2424 lets a homeowner postpone a trustee's sale by up to 90 days simply by listing the home for sale with a licensed agent. If selling is the right path to protect your equity, listing can create the time you need to actually complete that sale. The deadlines matter, so it's worth moving early.
