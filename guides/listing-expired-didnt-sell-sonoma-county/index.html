My House Didn't Sell and the Listing Expired — What Should I Do?

An expired listing doesn't mean your home won't sell. It almost always means one fixable thing was off. Here's how to find it and relaunch.

Short answer

An expired listing does not mean your home is unsellable. It almost always comes down to one of four things: price, marketing and presentation, access, or condition. The move isn't to relist at the same price with the same plan — it's to figure out which one held you back, fix it, and relaunch stronger.

The four real reasons a home doesn't sell

  • Price. The most common one. If the price was above recent comparable sales in your Sonoma County neighborhood, buyers quietly skipped it. Buyers touring but not offering is usually a price signal too. And sitting at the same number for months without adjusting is itself a decision, even when it never feels like one — the market gives you feedback every week.
  • Marketing and presentation. Weak photos, thin online exposure, or a listing that didn't reach enough qualified buyers. In a small buyer pool, presentation and reach do a lot of the work.
  • Access. The one almost nobody checks. Listing agents control showing access more than most sellers realize, and a buyer’s agent who calls twice without hearing back simply shows their client something else. You never find out it happened — the showing just never appears on your schedule.
  • Condition. Repairs or dated features that turned buyers away, or local factors that need to be addressed head-on in the marketing rather than left as a question mark.

How to diagnose which one it was

Look at four things from your last listing: your price versus what actually sold nearby, your showing activity (lots of showings but no offers points to price or condition; few showings points to price or marketing), your showing access (how quickly requests were answered, and whether anyone was ever turned away), and any buyer feedback your agent collected. Together those usually point straight at the real problem.

The mistake to avoid

Relisting immediately with the same price and the same plan — that just repeats the same result. The other version is dropping the price without fixing the marketing or the photos, so you leave money on the table when the real issue was exposure. Diagnose first, then relaunch.

A real example

A Sonoma County home expired after months with almost no showings. The price was close to right, but the photos and online presence were weak. We reshot it, rebuilt the marketing, corrected the price slightly, and relaunched — and it went under contract with fresh buyer interest.
