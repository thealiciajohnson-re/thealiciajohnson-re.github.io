How to Move Up to a Bigger Home in Sonoma County (When You Already Own One)

You're out of space and ready for the next home — but you already own one. Here's how to trade up without owning two homes at once, or none.

Short answer

If you already own a home and need more space, you have two paths: sell first, then buy, or buy first, then sell. The right one comes down to three things — how much equity you have, whether you can carry two payments for a short stretch, and how competitive your buy-side price range is right now. Most people start by shopping listings. That's the wrong order. You start with your numbers.

Know your local factors (Sonoma County)

  • Your equity is your biggest lever. Many longtime Sonoma County owners are sitting on significant equity. That equity funds the down payment on the bigger home — so the real first step is a current value on the home you own, not shopping listings.
  • Price band matters. Move-up inventory in Windsor, Rohnert Park, and Petaluma tends to move faster and more affordably than Healdsburg or Sonoma proper. Your target town shapes your strategy and timeline.
  • Contingent offers can work here — if they're written well. In a balanced market, a clean offer with a sale contingency is often accepted, especially when your current home is priced right and close to list-ready.
  • Financing bridges exist. A bridge loan or a HELOC on your current home can let you buy first and sell after, so you move once instead of twice. Whether it pencils out depends on your rate and equity.

The two paths, quickly

Sell first, then buy

You know your exact budget and you're a strong, non-contingent buyer. Trade-off: you may need a short-term rental or a rent-back from your buyer while you shop.

Buy first, then sell

Using a bridge loan, a HELOC, or a sale contingency, you move once and never live in limbo. Trade-off: you carry two payments briefly and need the equity or financing to support it.

The mistake to avoid

The costly one is guessing. Two versions: writing a non-contingent offer on the bigger home before you're certain your current home will sell at the number you're counting on — or listing your home before you have a realistic plan for where you'll land, then scrambling under a closing clock. Both come from doing the moves in the wrong order for your numbers. Map the money first, then move.

A real example

A couple in Rohnert Park had strong equity but couldn't carry two mortgages. We listed and went under contract on their home first, then wrote a clean offer in Windsor with a rent-back — so they only moved once. Both closings landed in the same week.
