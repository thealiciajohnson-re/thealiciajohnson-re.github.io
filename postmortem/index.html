A listing post-mortem

Your home didn't sell. That's information, not a verdict.

Most homes that come off the market unsold are good homes. Something in the plan didn't work — and it is almost always one of four things.

I'm a Sonoma County agent, and I work almost entirely with homes that didn't sell the first time around. I've seen what goes wrong on the second listing — and how to keep it from happening twice.

When I look at a listing that expired, these are the four things that stand out. Read them honestly against your own. One of them is usually the whole story.

The four things I look for

In the order they usually cost you money

  1. How the home was presented

    I know it's your house, and you live in it. But everybody who walks through looks at it from a different angle than you do. A room full of furniture you love reads as a small room to a buyer who's never been there.

    Decluttering isn't an insult to your taste. It's the cheapest thing on this list and it moves the needle more than almost anything else.

  2. How hard it was to actually get in

    This is the one that genuinely bothers me. Listing agents have far more control over access to a property than they should — and buyers don't fight for showings, they just move on to the next house.

    I have called listing agents five times about a serious buyer and never gotten a call back. If that happened even twice while you were on the market, you never found out about it. You can't sell to someone who couldn't get through the door.

  3. Where the price started

    The first two weeks are the most attention your listing will ever get. If the number was wrong out of the gate, you spent your best traffic proving it — and the showings that followed were mostly buyers using your home to justify buying a different one.

  4. Whether the price ever moved

    Sitting at the wrong number for months is a decision, even when nobody frames it as one. The market gives you feedback every single week. The question I'd ask about your last listing is simple: when the feedback came in, did anything actually change?

If I walk through it in person, I can usually tell you within about thirty seconds what went right and what went wrong.

That's not a sales pitch, it's just pattern recognition — it's what I do all week. And I'd rather tell you the honest version now than watch the same listing expire twice.
